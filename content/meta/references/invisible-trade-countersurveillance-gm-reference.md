## Countersurveillance: player instructions

Countersurveillance helps you assess whether someone is watching the party
and what seems to interest them, using evidence your character can access.
These are provisional trial rules. The GM will confirm when the trial begins;
check your relevant skills and abilities together before using them.

### Say what you want to learn

Describe your question and how you investigate it. Keep it specific enough to
answer through the actions you describe: who or what you are observing, over
which part of a journey or interaction, and what you are looking for.

You can attempt a plausible approach without Countersurveillance proficiency.
The GM decides whether a check is needed. Obvious information does not require
a roll.

### Use the abilities you already have

The GM identifies the appropriate ability and eligible skills from your
approach. If an existing skill applies, you can choose it instead of
Countersurveillance, keeping any Expertise you have in that skill.

Choose one eligible skill before rolling. Having both proficiencies does not
add your proficiency bonus twice or grant Advantage. Expertise in another
skill does not automatically transfer to Countersurveillance. Spells, class
features and specific detection rules retain their normal conditions and
limits; this skill does not reveal every observer or defeat magic by itself.

### Work together

For a shared question, choose a lead. Other characters can contribute by
creating an opportunity to observe, taking a distinct useful action, or using
Help when eligible. Help requires the proficiency used for the assisted check,
being close enough and being able to assist, under its normal rules.

A useful contribution need not involve a roll or grant a bonus. Everyone
rolling the same question is not a separate contribution.

### Decide what to do with the result

A successful assessment gives you a useful finding supported by the available
evidence. It may establish someone's apparent focus without revealing their
motive or employer.

A failed assessment normally means you cannot reach a reliable conclusion.
It does not prove that you are safe or automatically mean you have been
noticed. Consequences depend on what you did and the circumstances.

You choose your response: proceed, change plans, seek different evidence,
approach someone or withdraw. Further attempts require a meaningful change in
approach, evidence or circumstances, subject to any specific rule. Assessing
attention does not automatically evade it.

### Learn through play

After a relevant scene, the GM will briefly ask what your character learned.
Name the scene and explain the lesson in your own words. You can pass and
return to a remembered scene at level-up. Successful, failed and diceless
actions can all support learning, including contributions by someone who did
not make the assessment roll.

The GM records proposed learning for review; a note grants no immediate
benefit. At level-up, the GM confirms meaningful learning. Across the trial's
two skills, Countersurveillance and Asset Handling, you can earn up to two
points at that review, with at most one point in each skill. One point grants
proficiency in that skill. There is no automatic starting award or guaranteed
point for participating.

Earlier played scenes can count at the first review within the same cap;
there are no extra awards for previous levels. The same learning is not
credited repeatedly. Further points remain attached to their skill;
specialisation benefits are still to be decided. An unresolved candidate does
not delay ordinary level-up or undisputed awards, increase the cap, or
guarantee later credit.

### Tell us how it feels

We will review the trial at the first level-up and, if continuing, after two
further campaign sessions. Raise confusing rulings, uneven opportunities or
disruptions sooner. Rules may change between sessions, but events already
played remain intact. Before replacing an earned benefit, the GM and affected
player must agree an equivalent; until then, the earned benefit remains.
Ending the trial's observation period does not remove earned benefits.
