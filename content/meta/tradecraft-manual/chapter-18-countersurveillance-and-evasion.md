**Draft chapter.** Recognising and responding to hostile observation.

## The Errand and the Meeting

A courier is to meet a source after collecting a repaired boot and buying provisions. During those errands he notices a traveller whom he also saw outside his lodging. The recurrence deserves attention. Both men may simply have business in the same small market.

The courier must decide whether to continue before the uncertainty is resolved. If the meeting would expose someone whose connection to him is otherwise unknown, postponement may be justified by the possible cost. It need not be presented afterwards as proof that he was followed.

Ordinary errands consume daylight, money and patience. A conspicuously prolonged journey may itself attract notice, while an observer may remain attentive throughout a dull afternoon. Neither a familiar face nor the failure to notice one settles the question of surveillance.

The source must also be considered. Someone left waiting may take risks to discover what happened. Preparation should account for a missed meeting without requiring the waiting person to investigate the courier's absence.
