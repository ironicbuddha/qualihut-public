**Draft chapter.** Couriers, dead drops, recognition signals and secret correspondence.

## A Mark with an Agreed Meaning

A chalk stroke on a yard wall may mean nothing to a passer-by. To two people who have agreed its meaning, it may indicate that a message awaits collection. Most of the information lies in the prior agreement.

The wall belongs to other people's lives. Rain may wash it, a mason may renew it, and a child may add another stroke. An absent mark leaves several explanations open. A present one does not, by itself, establish who made it. Treat the signal according to what it can support, rather than allowing a small mark to acquire the authority of a signed dispatch.

## The Container Has Other Visitors

Suppose a packet is concealed among broken pots in a neglected corner. Its sender expects passers-by to leave the rubbish alone. The potter's apprentice has been told to clear that corner before the next delivery.

Separating deposit from collection can spare two correspondents a meeting. It also leaves the packet unattended in a place that continues to be used. Rain, animals, scavengers and ordinary maintenance do not respect the arrangement. Concealment must be considered alongside loss, damage and mistaken collection; distaste for a hiding place offers no guarantee that it will remain untouched.

## Writing That Has Yet to Appear

An apothecary demonstrates an ink whose dried marks become visible under a particular treatment. Before entrusting a report to it, the correspondent needs to know whether the recipient can obtain that treatment and whether the writing survives the paper, damp and delay of the intended journey.

Hidden writing conceals the presence of a message. Once revealed, its words may be perfectly legible. A cipher addresses a separate question: who can interpret those words? Neither arrangement establishes the sender's identity merely by existing.

Nor does secret writing confer access to protected correspondence. An operative who cannot open a sealed dispatch has gained no such ability by learning to conceal words upon a sheet.
