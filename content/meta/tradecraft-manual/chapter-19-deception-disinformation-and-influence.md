## Delay Dressed as Diligence

A guild examiner returns a repair petition for another witness's mark. When the witness attends, he questions the wording of a measurement already accepted. Each objection can be made to sound conscientious. Meanwhile, the damaged sluice remains closed and the mills below it stand idle.

The delay may be deliberate. It may also arise from uncertainty, fear of blame or a genuine defect in the petition. Repeated obstruction is an observable pattern; hostile intent requires further evidence. Examine which objections survive correction and whose interests the delay serves without treating either as sufficient proof on its own.

An operative who encourages such delay must reckon with more than the intended pressure upon the guild. Mill workers lose wages, grain spoils and households pay more for flour. The procedure provides an appearance of diligence. It cannot confine the consequences to the intended adversary.
